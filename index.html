02HK Undergraduate Financial Planners of the Year 2026Sep–Nov 2026

Built to Last: a 40-year plan for a HK$24.4M family

Investment Lead, Team Axis · Top 12 finalists

Left to their own devices, the Wongs sell their home at 83 in half of all futures. Five decisions made now lift the chance their money lasts to 97%.

62→97%chance money lasts to age 89
HK$45Ka year saved in fund fees
11→25%ESG share, like-for-like risk
  • Wrote the family investment policy: gave each part of the HK$7.7M portfolio a job (a 12-month reserve, a funded university pot, and a portfolio for each parent sized to their own risk: 40% and 60% equities).
  • Moved the family from active funds at ~1.5% a year to broad index funds at ~0.5%, saving HK$45K a year.
  • Raised ESG from 11% to 25% through like-for-like swaps, each fund required to pass two independent ESG ratings; capped crypto per person and kept it on HKEX-listed ETFs or SFC-licensed platforms.
  • Stress-tested the plan across 10,000 simulated markets, including job loss at 60 and a decade of low returns.

03Cathay Hackathon 2026Sep–Nov 2026 · Hong Kong

GateSense: when should a gate stop waiting for a late passenger?

Team Moodeng · Top 40 of Round 1

Don't decide "wait or offload". Decide a deadline, price every possible one in a single currency, and keep the cheapest.

−78%$1,555 vs $6,918, worked example
20,000simulated futures per deadline
€166cost per minute a plane is held
  • Designed three probabilistic models: will the passenger make it (and when), how long the bag takes to remove, and what each outcome costs Cathay.
  • Put delay, missed connections, curfew risk and customer lifetime value into one cost function, so a Gold member and a first-time flyer get different deadlines.
  • Ran a Monte Carlo of 20,000 futures for each candidate deadline with common random numbers, so only the decision differs; hard safety rules are never traded off.
